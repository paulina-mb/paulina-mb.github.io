How I work

Listen first, then decide with data

When I was promoted to lead former peers with far more years in the industry, I started by learning from them, then made decisions on evidence.

Rooted in PDCA: plan before you act · Root cause analysis: find the real cause

Measure what matters

SLA, MTTR, recurring incidents, NPS and CSAT: if a client feels it, I measure it, and I build the dashboard when there isn't one.

Rooted in ITIL 4: service level management · NPS: client loyalty

Improve a little, every day

Ticket trends become small, steady improvements, and the same problem should not come back twice.

Rooted in Kaizen: small, continuous improvement · Lean Six Sigma: less waste and variation

Bring options, not just problems

When I raise an issue, I bring the analysis with it: why it is happening, the options, and what each one would take in cost and effort.

Rooted in ITIL 4: start where you are · Cost-benefit analysis: compare the options

Build what's missing

If the team is losing time, I find out why and fix it, even when nobody asked for it.

Rooted in Lean: eliminate waste

Calm, clear communication

During a major incident, clients should always know what happened, what we are doing and when they will hear from us next.

Rooted in ITIL 4: major incident management

Two 1:1s with each person

One on results, one on growth. People on my team moved on to technical, commercial and project roles, and I trained the next ones.

Rooted in Coaching: grow your people · Performance management: clear goals and feedback

Governance clients can trust

Processes that pass an audit and still feel simple for the people who use them every day.

Rooted in ISO/IEC 20000: service management · ISO/IEC 27001: information security

Deliver in short cycles

Onboardings, migrations and improvements move in small steps, with clear milestones and risks in view.

Rooted in Agile: respond to change · Scrum: work in short sprints

Stay until it's closed

A client once asked for a different manager. I kept helping him anyway, and he kept calling me for help.

Rooted in ITIL 4: focus on value · Problem management: stop repeat issues
